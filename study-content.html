Study IV In Review

Social Media Content & Pornography-Related Urges

An experimental study on sexualised social media content, pornography-related desire, and the role of meaningful content.

Overview

Sexualised content is widespread on social media, appearing in advertising, influencer posts, and user-generated material. This study asks whether brief exposure to such content, without explicit imagery, can momentarily increase pornography-related desire and behavioural intention, and whether adding meaningful, thought-provoking content to a feed may weaken that effect.

The study used an experimental design: participants were randomly assigned to browse one of three researcher-curated social media feeds (sexualised, combined sexualised/meaningful, or neutral), and their pornography-related desire, behavioural intention, and self-efficacy were measured with brief questions before and after browsing. An online sample of 857 participants, mostly young adults, allowed us to explore whether individual differences, such as problematic pornography use, problematic social media use, emotional problems, and sex, shaped how people responded.
